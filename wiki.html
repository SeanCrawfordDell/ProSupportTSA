How the tools fit together

The tools follow the life of a support case. You can use any of them on their own, but data flows forward when you use them in order.

  1. 1 · CaptureCase Notes: case details and a dated note for each day of work.
  2. 2 · CollectEvidence stage and the log collection helper.
  3. 3 · ResolveRecord the fix, verify it, plan prevention.
  4. 4 · EscalateEscalate to DE imports your case and scores readiness.
  5. 5 · LearnKnowledge draft and Support Trends.

What you should know first

  • Your data stays in your browser. Notes are saved in this browser on this computer. Nothing is uploaded unless you choose to copy, email, back up to a folder, or send something to an AI tool. See Privacy and security.
  • Notes autosave every ten seconds, but browser storage can be erased by clearing site data. Set up a backup folder early. See Backup and recovery.
  • Scores find gaps; they do not judge accuracy. The note quality score and escalation readiness score are rule-based checks for missing or vague information. They cannot verify that your technical conclusions are correct.
  • AI is optional. Every AI feature shows you what will be sent and needs an explicit click.

Quick start

Your first case in about ten minutes

The fastest way to learn Case Notes is to explore the built-in sample cases, then start one of your own.

  1. Open Case Notes. From the home page choose Open Case Notes, or use Case Management → Case Notes in the navigation. A message reading “Opening case history…” appears for a moment while the page checks that no other tab is editing.
  2. Load the sample cases. Open Tutorial & examples in the top bar and choose Load Example. It adds ten labelled sample cases to Recent cases and opens the main one: a PowerEdge R750 Hyper-V host whose virtual machines lose network connectivity after a NIC driver update. Your own cases are not changed.
  3. Take the guided tour. Choose Tutorial Demo from the same menu. The 33-step tour highlights each area using the sample case and returns you to your own case when it ends. Use Next, Back, or Skip demo.
  4. Look around the sample. Click the three date tabs, open Case Summary, switch between the four workflow stages, and read the Note quality score at the bottom. Notice the red and yellow cards in Recent cases and the “2 overdue · 3 due within 4 hours” pill in the page header.
  5. Start your own case. Choose + New Case (or Start New Case in an empty workspace). Fill in the Case Details, then write your first note. The time tracker starts automatically.
  6. Record actions with their results. For every step, write what you did and what happened (“Result: …”). The quality score and any later escalation depend on outcomes, not just actions.
  7. Work through the workflow. Use 1 · Triage, 2 · Evidence, 3 · Verify resolution, and 4 · Knowledge draft as the case progresses.
  8. Close the loop. Choose Copy to Lightning to copy the day’s record, which also stops the timer (see Copy, email, print and export for copying the whole case).
  9. Protect your work. Choose Set Up Backups in the banner, or Settings (gear) → Backup & Restore → Choose Backup Folder, and pick a folder in your work OneDrive.

Your first-day checklist

  • Loaded the sample cases and opened each date tab and workflow stage.
  • Completed the Tutorial Demo.
  • Created a case with a valid Service Tag, Service Request number, OS version, and Log Location.
  • Recorded at least one action and its result.
  • Chose a backup folder so a second copy of your notes exists.
  • Tried Escalate to DE on the sample case to see the readiness check (see DE Escalation Request).
The samples are safe to change

Edit them, archive them, or delete them like any other case. Loading the example again asks before resetting them. They are labelled “Sample ·”, are left out of Support Trends, and never send desktop notifications. If Recent cases does not have room for ten more cases, nothing is loaded and you are told how many to archive; your real cases are never archived to make room.

Cases and case details

Creating, finding, and organizing cases

A case is one service request. It holds shared details (the system, the OS, where the logs are) plus any number of dated notes.

Create a case

  1. Choose + New Case in the workspace header (or Start New Case when nothing is open).
  2. Fill in Case Details (below). Everything saves automatically.
  3. Write your first dated note. The time tracker starts as soon as you begin typing.
Why can’t I edit?

Only one browser tab can edit Case Notes at a time so two tabs can never overwrite each other. Other tabs show “Read-only while another tab is editing Case Notes.” A read-only tab becomes editable by itself when the editing tab closes, or choose Take over editing here to switch immediately: the other tab is asked to save first, then becomes read-only.

OS/Solution choices

Azure Local, Azure Stack Hub, Debian, ESX, Nutanix, OpenShift, Redhat, Systems Management, Ubuntu, Windows Server, and No OS. Some choices reveal an extra Product/Application field. The OS also drives the log collection plan and which issue types are offered.

Rearrange or hide fields

  • Unlock layout (in the Case Details heading) lets you drag fields, or focus a field’s ⋮⋮ grip and use the arrow keys. While it is unlocked, fields cannot be edited. Done saves the order for every case; Cancel or Esc puts it back.
  • Each field also has a Show checkbox. Hidden fields appear dimmed so you can bring them back. The first time you hide one, a notice asks you to acknowledge that the information is still needed and will be recorded another way.
  • Hidden fields keep their values. Copy to Lightning, email, and escalation output still include them, and they still count in the quality score. OS/Solution, Notes, and Action Plan / Next Steps cannot be hidden.
  • A Show or hide fields button under Case Details lists what is hidden. The same controls exist in Customize Site Options.

Archive and Trash

  • When you pass 100 recent cases, older unpinned cases move to Archive automatically instead of being discarded. If every case is pinned, the oldest one still moves to Archive.
  • Archived and trashed cases keep their notes, screenshots, and versions. Choose the collection, open a case to read it, and use Restore to bring it back.
  • Trash is kept until you permanently delete a case. That cannot be undone.
Storage is shared

Recent cases, Archive, Trash, and saved versions all live in the same browser storage, which is not unlimited. Screenshots take the most room. A backup folder (see Backup and recovery) is your safety net.

Dated notes and the editor

One case, one note per day of work

Instead of one ever-growing note, each day of work gets its own dated entry. Case details, screenshots, workflow, and total time are shared across the whole case.

Dated entries

  • + New note (beside the Pop out button) starts another dated entry for the same case. Use + New Case only for a different service request.
  • Tabs show each entry’s creation date in your local time zone. Several entries on the same day also show a time and entry number.
  • Editing an old entry never changes its date. Switching tabs saves your current work first; if a save fails you stay on the current entry with your edits intact.
  • Each entry has its own Notes and Action Plan / Next Steps. Case details, screenshots, workflow, and total time are shared.

Case Summary

The Case Summary tab shows the shared case details once, then every dated note and action plan from oldest to newest. Each dated section collapses. It is a formatted read-through of what you wrote, not an AI summary. Select a date tab to resume editing. Its Copy case summary button copies the whole case (see Copy, email, print and export).

HTML email, Print/PDF, the escalation handoff, and AI review include the complete dated history, regardless of which tab is selected.

The editor

  • Formatting toolbar: Bold, Italic, Underline, Bullets, Numbered list, Link, and Clear formatting.
  • Screenshots: paste with Ctrl V (⌘ V on Mac). Click an image, then drag its bottom-right corner to resize it. With a resize handle focused, use the arrow keys.
  • Slash commands: type /st abc1234 on its own line and press Enter to fill in Service Tag without leaving Notes. Every case field has a code; type / to see them. See Slash commands in Notes.
  • Collapsible sections: Case Details, Notes, and Action Plan / Next Steps each collapse with their arrow. The state is remembered.
  • Jump to Notes (Alt+Shift+J) scrolls to the editor. If you mostly write notes, Customize Site Options can show Notes and Action Plan above the workflow and Case Details.
  • Pop out ↗ opens a compact window with the editor so you can write beside a customer call. Only one window edits at a time; Return to case saves before returning. If your browser blocks the pop-up, you are offered a compact view in the same tab.

Slash commands in Notes

Fill in case fields without leaving the editor

Type a short command such as /st abc1234 on its own line in Notes and press Enter. The Service Tag field is filled in, and the line stays in your notes as Service Tag: ABC1234. Use it to capture details while you talk to the customer, without moving between fields.

Try it in 30 seconds

  1. Open any case (or Tutorial & examples → Load Example) and click at the end of the Notes editor.
  2. On a new line, type /st abc1234 and press Enter.
  3. Look at Service Tag in Case Details: it now shows ABC1234. In Notes, the line reads Service Tag: ABC1234, and a new line is ready for you.
  4. Type /os win and press Tab. OS/Solution changes to Windows Server, and the cursor stays at the end of that line.
  5. Open the Case Summary tab. Both values are listed with the case details.

How it works

When you press Enter or Tab, Case Notes looks at the line the cursor is on:

  1. Is it a command? It must start with /, then a known code, a space, and a value. The cursor must be at the end of the line. Anything else is ordinary text, and the key works as usual.
  2. Is the value valid? Text fields accept anything. Dropdown fields must match one of their options (see Dropdown fields). If the value is not valid, nothing changes and the reason appears under Notes.
  3. Fill in the field. The value goes into the field exactly as if you had typed it there. It autosaves, starts the timer if it was stopped, and shows in Case Summary, Copy to Lightning, email, the escalation handoff, and the quality score.
  4. Rewrite the line. The command is replaced with Field label: value, so the note still reads well for the next person. Ctrl Z brings back the typed command, but the field keeps its new value.
A command replaces the field

Running /st again replaces the Service Tag rather than adding to it. The same is true for long fields such as Issue Description and Recent change. Type the full text you want, or edit the field directly.

Dropdown fields accept part of an option

You don’t need to type the whole option. Case Notes looks for a match in this order and stops at the first step that finds exactly one option:

  1. The whole option, ignoring capitals: /os ubuntu. For Customer Country, the two-letter code also counts: /cc gb.
  2. The start of an option: /os win → Windows Server.
  3. Any part of an option: /os stack → Azure Stack Hub.

If the text matches more than one option, nothing changes and the message under Notes lists the matches so you can add a word. If it matches none, the message lists every option.

The Issue type list depends on OS/Solution. Changing OS/Solution can reset Issue type to that OS’s default. Run /os first, then /it. Product/Application is only available while OS/Solution is Systems Management.

Custom fields

Fields you add in Customize Site Options get a command automatically: a slash followed by the field’s ID in lower case. A custom field with the ID Asset uses /asset 4471. Type / to see the codes your custom fields were given. If a custom field’s ID matches a code in the table above, for example a custom field named st, that field gets no command and the built-in one wins.

Rules to remember

  • One command per line, at the start of the line. Called customer /st abc1234 is just text.
  • The cursor must be at the end of the line when you press Enter or Tab. Commands don’t run when you press Shift+Enter, Ctrl, or Alt.
  • Pasting doesn’t run commands. After pasting a command line, click at its end and press Enter.
  • Notes only. Commands work in the Notes editor, including the Pop out window. They don’t work in Action Plan / Next Steps or in a case that is read-only (another tab is editing, or Case Summary is open).
  • Values that are too long are cut to the field’s limit (for example 250 characters for OS version / build).
  • Hidden fields still work. A field hidden from Case Details is filled in and keeps its value; you just don’t see it on the page.

Note quality score

A live, rule-based check for missing and vague information

At the bottom of every case, a score from 0 to 100 updates as you write and scores every dated entry of the case together. Its job is to show you the biggest gaps first.

85+ Strong 60–84 Needs detail Below 60 Incomplete

Next steps15Actions with an owner and a date, from the Action Plan or the follow-up tracker.

Rules that keep a note below “Strong”

  • Placeholder or repeated text. A field that reads as filler (few distinct words, repeated sentences, lorem-ipsum, or keyboard-mashing) earns nothing and is flagged.
  • Copied fields. If two scored fields are nearly identical, the earlier one keeps the credit and the later one is flagged by name.
  • Steps without outcomes. Credit is proportional to the share of steps with an outcome term such as “Result”, “Observed”, or “confirmed”.
  • Format mismatches in Service Tag (5–10 letters and digits), Service Request (6+ digits), OS version (contains a number), or Log Location (link or path) earn half credit.
  • Leftover prompt text from the retired templates earns no troubleshooting credit. A prompt label followed by a very short answer is ignored.
What the score cannot do

It finds missing information. It does not verify that your diagnosis is right or replace your judgment, and it is not a team metric. Hidden Case Details fields still count.

A fast route to 100

  1. Read To raise the score. It lists the biggest gaps first.
  2. Fix the format checks in Case Details (they are quick wins).
  3. Add “Result:” to each troubleshooting step.
  4. Set an owner and due date in Set follow-up, or write them in the Action Plan.
  5. Tick the evidence checklist items you have genuinely reviewed.

Backup and recovery

Keeping your notes safe

Notes save automatically in your browser. A backup folder gives you a second copy that survives clearing browser data, moving to a new PC, or a mistake.

Do this on day one

Browser storage is erased if you clear site data or reset the browser. Until you choose a backup folder, that is the only copy of your notes. A banner at the top of the page reminds you, and Remind me in a week hides it for seven days.

How saving works

  • Autosave: changes are saved in this browser every ten seconds. The workspace shows Saved when nothing is pending, and a Retry save button if a save fails.
  • Storage: Recent cases (up to 100), Archive, Trash, and saved versions share browser storage on this computer and this browser profile. Another browser or profile has its own data.
  • One editor: only one tab can edit at a time, which protects notes from being overwritten.

Quick start (about 30 seconds)

  1. Open Settings (gear) → Backup & Restore and choose Choose Backup Folder.
  2. Pick a folder inside your work OneDrive (Documents is a good choice) so OneDrive also keeps a cloud copy. Use an approved location.
  3. Approve the browser’s permission prompt. Case Notes creates a ProSupportToolsBackup folder inside it and makes the first backup immediately.
  4. The status card turns green and shows Backed up just now.

Folder backup needs Chrome or Edge. In other browsers use Download a copy instead (below).

How automatic backup works

  • A few seconds after you change a note (and at most every 30 seconds), the latest copy is refreshed. The page must be open: backup is not a background service.
  • Once an hour while you work, a dated automatic snapshot is saved, so you can go back to how things looked earlier in the day or last week.
  • Back Up Now saves immediately and adds a manual backup, which is never deleted automatically. Use it before risky edits or at the end of a case.
  • Before anything is replaced or removed (a restore, removing a custom field, resetting fields) a safety copy is saved first. Safety copies are never deleted automatically.
  • Settings (fields, toolbox, preferences) are backed up with the history, and also within about a minute of any setting change, even if you have not edited a note.

If the browser closes and reopens it may need permission again. The card says Backups are paused; select Reconnect Backup Folder and approve.

Restore

  1. Open Backup & Restore → Restore…
  2. Choose Case history or Settings at the top. On the Settings tab, a copy marked same as your current settings is already active, so its Restore button is disabled.
  3. Pick a backup. The list is grouped as Most recent, Manual backups, Safety copies, and Automatic snapshots, newest first, with a friendly time and size.
  4. Select Restore, read the confirmation (it states how many cases will be replaced), and confirm. Your current data is saved as a safety copy first, so if you picked the wrong one, restore the safety copy.
  • On a new computer or browser: choose the same OneDrive folder. Case Notes sees the existing backups and asks whether to Restore from these backups or Keep my current notes and back up here. It never overwrites the folder silently; if you close the question, backups stay paused until you decide.
  • Restore from a file… accepts a file you downloaded or copied from another computer. Case-history and settings files are told apart automatically.
  • A history restore replaces the complete history, including Archive, Trash, and versions. After a settings restore the page reloads once so every setting takes effect.

What settings backups include

  • Toolbox: shortcut links (up to four), button order and colours, launcher icon, sizes, circle and animation options.
  • Case fields: custom fields, their order, and which fields are hidden; and your own AI prompts.
  • Preferences: theme, floating action bar, Grid/List view, site effects and options, collapsed sections and Recent cases panel, pinned site resources, and the backup retention choice.

Not included: the temporary dragged position of the toolbox, and the browser’s folder permission.

Automatic cleanup (retention)

Under Automatic cleanup, choose how long to keep automatic snapshots: 7 days, 30 days (default), 90 days, or Forever.

  • Cleanup runs after each hourly snapshot; Clean Up Now runs it on demand and tells you how much space it freed.
  • Snapshots from the last 24 hours are all kept; older ones are thinned to one per day for up to 30 days, then one per week up to your limit.
  • The newest snapshot, the latest copy, manual backups, safety copies, and any file the app did not create are never removed.
  • Settings copies follow their own rule: the newest 5 automatic copies from today and the newest 1 from yesterday are kept.

Best practices and recipes

Habits and step-by-step workflows

The tools reward specific, outcome-focused notes. These habits make every case easier to hand off.

Principles

  • Be specific. Name the component, the version, the exact error, and the time.
  • Pair every action with its result. “Checked X” is half a sentence.
  • Write for the next reader. Assume the next person has never seen the case.
  • Record evidence location, not just that evidence exists.
  • Always leave a next step with an owner and a date. Your future self will thank you when the card turns red.
  • Close completely. Verify by repeating the failing scenario, then plan prevention.

  1. + New Case. Fill in all case details and check the format of the Service Tag, SR, and Log Location.
  2. Triage: set OS/Solution, Issue type, Service Impact, and Recent change.
  3. Write the first dated note: customer description, scope, and what you checked with results. While on the call, capture details as you hear them with slash commands such as /st, /sr and /chg.
  4. Evidence: open the log collection helper (and verify what it suggests) and decide what to request. Set a follow-up for when the customer should respond.

  1. Open the case, then + New note (Alt+Shift+N). Use Case Summary to re-read earlier days.
  2. Write what changed, what you did, and what happened.
  3. Update the follow-up owner, status, and due date.

  1. Select the header pill, or set Show cases to Overdue follow-ups.
  2. Open each red card first, then the yellow ones.
  3. Do the follow-up, add a dated note of what happened, then choose Follow-up done and pick Schedule a new follow-up, No follow-up, or Case is complete.

Recipe: repeat contact

  1. Search Recent cases by Service Tag or SR to find the earlier case (tick Search Archive and Trash too).
  2. Read its Case Summary to see what was tried.
  3. In the new case, enter the previous SR in Related / repeat case, and note what is different now.
  4. Check Support Trends to see if other cases share the same configuration.

Recipe: escalate to a Domain Engineer

  1. Make sure troubleshooting steps have results and logs are collected.
  2. Choose Escalate to DE. Complete severity, business impact, affected systems, and timeline.
  3. Add action and result rows for anything not in your notes.
  4. Choose Review escalation and clear all blocking issues. Aim comfortably above 75.
  5. Copy escalation data and submit it. Use ← Return to Case Notes and record the DE’s findings back in your case.

Recipe: shift handoff

  1. Open Handoff Summary, fill in business impact and remaining questions, and generate.
  2. Copy it, and make sure the follow-up has an owner and due time.

Recipe: close a case and capture what you learned

  1. Verify resolution: fix, verification test and result, prevention plan, customer confirmation.
  2. Mark verified and completed, then Copy to Lightning for the day’s notes, or Copy case summary for the whole case.
  3. Build a knowledge draft, redact it, and export it to an approved destination.

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers

Data and privacy

Where are my notes stored?

In your browser’s local storage on this computer, for this site. Nothing is uploaded unless you copy, email, send to an AI tool, or choose a backup folder.

I cleared my browser data. Are my notes gone?

Yes, unless you had a backup folder. Open Backup & Restore, choose the same folder, and pick Restore from these backups. See Backup and recovery.

Can I use the tools on another computer or browser?

Data does not follow you automatically. Choose the same backup folder (for example in OneDrive) on the other machine and restore from it.

Is customer data sent to AI automatically?

No. Copy to AI only copies a prompt to your clipboard. Send to Devin shows the prompt for review and needs a click. Neither runs on its own.

Cases and notes

Should I use + New Case or + New note?

+ New Case for a different service request; + New note for another day on the same request.

Why can’t I edit? The buttons are greyed out.

Another tab is editing Case Notes, or the history could not be read. Close the other tab, choose Take over editing here, or reload. See Troubleshooting.

What happens to cases after 100?

Older unpinned cases move to Archive automatically. Nothing is deleted.

What does Copy to Lightning copy?

Every case field plus only the notes and action plan from the dated notes made on the same day as the selected one. To copy the whole case, open Case Summary and choose Copy case summary.

The page shook when I copied. Is something wrong?

No, that is the copy confirmation, with a quiet click sound. Turn it off in Customize Site Options.

Where did Apply template go?

The templates feature was removed. The Issue type list still drives the evidence checklist and the log plan, and older cases and backups keep working.

Can I hide fields I never use?

Yes. Use Unlock layout in Case Details or Hide in Customize Site Options. Hidden fields keep their values and still appear in exports and the score.

Does the timer run while I am away?

It runs while tracking is on. Use Stop Tracking when you step away, or turn on the 15-minute away prompt in Customize Site Options (Chrome and Edge).

Can I fill in case fields from the Notes editor?

Yes. Type a slash command on its own line, for example /st abc1234 or /os win, and press Enter or Tab. Type / to see every code. See Slash commands in Notes.

I typed /st in Notes and nothing happened.

The command must be on its own line, with a value after the code, and the cursor at the end of the line when you press Enter. For dropdown fields, read the message under Notes: the value may match more than one option or none. See If a command doesn’t run.

Can I undo a bad edit?

Use Version History (up to ten saved versions per case) or restore a backup.

How do I recover a deleted case?

Open Filters, set Collection to Trash, open the case, and choose Restore (unless you permanently deleted it).

Scores

My note scores low but I know the work was done.

The score can only see what you wrote. Add outcomes, an owner and date, and correct any format issues listed under To raise the score.

Does 100 mean the diagnosis is right?

No. Scores measure completeness and specificity, not technical accuracy.

Why is my escalation “Not ready” with a high score?

Readiness needs 75+ and no blocking issues. A single vague or copied required field can block it.

AI and Devin

Do I need Devin to use the AI buttons?

No. Copy to AI works on its own. Devin is optional.

I cannot see the AI buttons.

On laptop-sized screens they are inside AI tools in the action bar. Select it to open them.

Send to Devin is greyed out.

The connection is not set up or not running. Open AI Settings and follow Connect Devin.

This wiki

Something looks different from what I see in the app.

The app changes over time. Use Request feature / Report bug in the top bar of any tool to tell us, and do not include customer data.

Can I print this wiki?

Yes. Use your browser’s print command; all pages print in order without the navigation.

Glossary

Terms used in the tools

Action bar
The row (or wide-screen rail) of copy, escalate, email, update, handoff, helper, and AI buttons.
Action Plan / Next Steps
The section of a dated note listing what happens next and who owns it.
Archive
Where older cases go once Recent cases passes 100. Archived cases keep everything and can be restored.
Automatic snapshot
A dated copy of your case history saved to the backup folder about once an hour while you work.
Backup folder
A folder you choose (ideally in OneDrive) where Case Notes keeps its backups, inside a ProSupportToolsBackup folder.
Case Summary
A read-only, formatted view of shared case details plus every dated note, oldest to newest.
Case Toolkit
The follow-up tracker, customer update generator, and handoff summary.
Copy to Lightning
Copies the case fields and the selected day’s notes so they can be pasted into the case system. Stops the timer.
DE
Domain Engineer: the senior technician an escalation is handed to.
Dated note (entry)
One day’s notes and action plan within a case, labelled by creation date.
Escalation readiness
A 100-point, rule-based score showing whether a DE has enough specific evidence to begin. Ready means 75+ and no blocking issues.
Evidence checklist
The Evidence stage list of items to collect, adapted to the OS and issue type.
Follow-up
An owner, due date and time, and status attached to a case. Red when overdue, yellow when due within 4 hours.
Knowledge draft
An editable article built from a case, for export to an approved notebook or vault. Never published automatically.
Log collection helper
A dialog that recommends which logs to collect for the case’s OS and issue type. Recommendations only, and still in development.
Manual backup / safety copy
Backups made by Back Up Now, and automatic copies made just before a restore or removal. Never deleted automatically.
Note quality score
A 0–100 rule-based check of a case’s notes for missing or vague information.
Obsidian vault
The Obsidian notebook folder that a knowledge draft can be sent to.
Pop out
A compact window for the notes editor.
Sample case
A labelled practice case added by Load Example. Not counted in Support Trends.
Service Tag
The identifier on a Dell system. The tools expect 5–10 letters and digits.
Slash command
A line in Notes such as /st abc1234 that fills in a case field when you press Enter or Tab. See Slash commands in Notes.
SR
Service Request. The tools expect a number of six or more digits.
Trash
Where deleted cases wait until you restore or permanently delete them.
TSR
Technical Support Report: a hardware and configuration collection exported from iDRAC.
Unlock layout
A mode that lets you drag, reorder, and show or hide Case Details fields directly on the page.
Version History
Up to ten previous saved versions of a case that you can preview and restore.

The tools do not send case data to a server of their own, and have no analytics.

Safe handling

  • Case notes, exports, backups, and prompts can contain customer information. Follow your organization’s retention and handling policies.
  • Backup files are not encrypted by the app. Use an approved, protected location. The app cannot confirm that OneDrive finished uploading.
  • Remove customer-identifying information from knowledge drafts and AI prompts.
  • Do not paste case data into bug reports or feature requests.

Built-in protections

  • Pages refuse to render inside another site’s frame to prevent clickjacking.
  • A strict Content Security Policy limits each page to its own scripts, styles, and images.
  • Rich text is sanitized before it is displayed or printed.
  • Imports and restores are validated before they change anything, and risky actions ask first or save a safety copy.
Known limitation: shared origin

Browsers separate storage by origin (scheme and host), not by path. Other pages published on the same host can in principle read the same storage. Use the tools only from a host you trust and avoid sharing the host with untrusted sites.
